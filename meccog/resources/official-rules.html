    MecCog Challenge Official Rules

    Version 1, November 2026. Effective November 17th, 2026.

    Please read these rules before registering. They are an agreement between you and the MecCog challenge organizers. By registering for a MecCog challenge you accept them.

    0. Terms for the current challenge

    Everything in this section changes from one MecCog challenge to the next. Everything after it is the standing rule set and does not.

    Term Current challenge

    1. Participation

    Participating in a MecCog challenge means submitting one or more Entries to that challenge. An Entry is a submission in the format the challenge page specifies, made within the challenge period.

    2. Eligibility

    MecCog aims to maximize eligibility. Anyone may take part, subject to the following.

    Individuals who have, or may have, access to privileged information about a challenge may not participate in it or advise other participants. This includes the challenge organizers, the members of the MecCog working group, and anyone providing scientific or technical advice to the organizers. Employees and trainees whose work is directly supervised by one of these individuals may submit Entries, but their Entries are not eligible for prizes where a challenge offers them. They remain eligible to be named on challenge publications.

    Where the organizers run their own systems on a challenge task, those results are published as a reference point and identified as such. They are not Entries and are not ranked against participants’ Entries.

    Individuals suspected of misconduct in a previous MecCog challenge, or banned from a MecCog challenge or from a platform needed to take part, are not eligible.

    Minors may participate, and by doing so represent that a parent or legal guardian has read and agreed to these rules.

    A challenge may add eligibility conditions of its own, which the challenge page will state.

    3. Registration

    Register once per challenge. Registering more than once, holding more than one account, or having someone else register in order to submit on your behalf may lead to all of your Entries being disqualified.

    Registration asks for an institutional or company email address. If you do not have one, complete the short description of who you are and what you do, which the organizers may follow up by email before activating the account. We ask for this so that we know who produced each Entry, not in order to publish it: section 13 covers what is made public.

    By registering you confirm that your account information is accurate. An account with information that is incorrect or misleading may be terminated and its Entries disqualified.

    4. Teams

    Every Entry belongs to a team. After registering, you may work alone as a team of one, create a team, or join an existing one. There is no maximum team size.

    Every team names a team leader, who is the point of contact for the organizers and is responsible for the team’s Entries. A team with no leader cannot submit.

    Each member of a team must be a registered participant. You may belong to more than one team, but creating a second team requires a written explanation, for example that your group has two genuinely different methods and wants both assessed. Creating several teams to multiply your submissions is not allowed.

    5. Use of other data and tools

    You may use any literature source, database, model, tool or service you wish, including commercial ones, unless the challenge page says otherwise. The obligation that comes with this is in section 10: your method must be public and reproducible by others, so a tool nobody else can run limits what your Entry can be credited with.

    6. Entries

    An Entry is submitted on the platform the challenge page names, in the format it specifies, and must pass the challenge validator. A submission that does not validate cannot be uploaded.

    Entries must arrive within the challenge period. An Entry submitted after the deadline may not be assessed. Once an Entry has been assessed it may not be altered without the organizers’ agreement, and altering an assessed Entry is treated as misconduct.

    A challenge sets a maximum number of Entries per team, stated in section 0.

    Every Entry names a public repository or archive holding a description of the method that produced it, and the commit or version it was built from. This is a condition of submission, not an optional extra, and an Entry without it does not validate. The method description must be detailed enough that another group could in principle reproduce it.

    Accepted Entries are given an acceptance number, shown on screen. Queries about an Entry must be accompanied by the corresponding acceptance number.

    All Entries are made public after the challenge results are announced, together with the associated methods. The team leader’s name and email address are removed from the Method sheet before an Entry is published. They are how the organizers reach the team, not part of what is published, and section 13 governs what is.

    7. Assessment

    This challenge has no ground truth. There is no withheld answer key, because what the literature contains is itself one of the things being measured. Assessment therefore differs from a challenge scored against held-out data, and the methods may develop as the project progresses.

    The initial assessment principles for each challenge are provided in advance, on the challenge page, without committing to a single ranking formula. Further considerations may be introduced once the results are in hand, as is the practice in CASP.

    The organizers’ assessment decisions are final.

    8. Misconduct and disqualification

    The following are treated as scientific misconduct, not as tactics, and the consequences are those set out below.

    Fabricating a finding, or attributing a finding to a paper or a location that does not contain it. Recording a value estimated from a figure as though the paper had printed it. Recording a value as absent from a paper that reports it. Removing findings that contradict the assigned hypothesis, or that show no effect, in order to produce a cleaner submission. Using false identities or multiple accounts to exceed the submission limit. Altering an assessed Entry. Misleading other participants.

    Cross-comparison between independent Entries makes most of these visible, which is part of why the comparison is done.

    Where the organizers conclude that misconduct has occurred, the Entry and the team are disqualified. Depending on the gravity, the individual or team may also be banned from future MecCog challenges.

    Participants agree not to disparage other participants, other teams, or the organizers. This does not prevent constructive criticism made in good faith and in a collegial manner.

    9. Authorship

    Publications arising from this and other MecCog Challenges will include participants as authors where appropriate. Where a participant earns authorship, the policy is as follows.

    An overview paper may be written for a challenge, or for a set of challenges together (not necessarily for each round). For these papers, every participating team in good standing in the challenges may be co-authors, listed under membership of the MecCog Challenge Consortium, on the understanding that they supply a description of their method for the appendix and answer a short survey about it.

    A team that does not wish to be named may decline, and in doing so grants permission for its method to be described in the paper.

    There may be additional invited author publications in journal special issues as the project proceeds.

    10. Methods, code, and intellectual property

    The organizers make no claim to ownership of your Entry or of any intellectual property associated with it. You keep the copyright in your code.

    Because the point of the project is to develop a working pipeline that the scientific community can use, an Entry must be in principle reproducible. Thus, the method used must be described in enough detail that another group could follow it, and will be made public, as section 6 requires. That is the condition of submission.

    Providing the code is encouraged and not required. A team that cannot release its code, because it is proprietary or for any other reason, may still submit and is assessed on the same footing, provided the method description meets the appropriate standard. If you are a company or have another reason to withhold your code, please email us at e4admin@tunc.org to obtain an exemption. Where a team does release code, it does so under an open source license of its choice that permits redistribution for research and development. Code released without a stated license is taken to be released under the MIT license, and by submitting without stating one you confirm that you are entitled to allow that.

    An Entry that depends on proprietary software or data that others cannot obtain may be submitted and assessed, and may be described in the challenge paper. What such an Entry cannot be credited with is reproducibility by others, and the challenge paper will say which Entries released their code and which did not.

    Participants who prefer not to release code may also make their system available for the organizers to run, where a challenge makes that practical.

    You are free to discuss your Entry with anyone and to contract with third parties, provided you do not enter into any obligation that conflicts with these rules.

    By submitting an Entry you grant the organizers the right to assess it, to have it assessed by people they designate, and to make it public after the challenge results are announced.

    11. Publicity

    Except where prohibited, taking part in a MecCog challenge is your consent to the organizers publishing the team name, the names of team members who have not declined under section 9, and the Entry itself, in connection with the challenge and its publications.

    12. Publishing your own results before the challenge paper

    If your team posts a preprint or submits a paper about your participation before the challenge overview paper appears, restrict it to your own work: the method you developed and your own result. Do not report the overall challenge results or any analysis of them. These restrictions end when the overview paper is published.

    13. What is public, and anonymity

    Team names and Entries are published after the results are announced, as section 6 states. The identity of the individuals on a team is known to the organizers through registration and is not published unless those individuals are named as authors under section 9.

    If you prefer that your performance not be publicly associated with you, register under your own identity and give your team a name that does not identify you. We understand that a poor result should not be usable against someone in a publication, a promotion, or a grant application, and participants are asked to respect that choice in others.

    The organizers make no other representation as to confidentiality. Do not include confidential or unpublished third-party information in an Entry.

    14. Your warranties

    By submitting an Entry you confirm, to the extent you are able, that the information in it is true and complete, that you are entitled to submit it on behalf of yourself or your team, and that the Entry:

    is your own work, or credits the work of others within it; contains no confidential information or trade secrets; infringes nobody’s intellectual property or other rights; contains no malicious code; and violates no applicable law.

    15. Limitation of liability

    The organizers, their institutions, and the funders and data contributors of a MecCog challenge are not liable for loss or damage arising from your participation, to the extent the law permits.

    The organizers are not responsible for technical failures outside their control, including platform outages and lost or late Entries.

    The organizers may cancel, modify, or suspend a challenge where a technical problem or a cause beyond their control affects its integrity.

    16. Changes to these rules

    The organizers may amend these rules. If they are amended while a challenge is open, participants will be notified and given the amended rules together with a list of what changed. Continuing to participate accepts the amendment. A participant who does not wish to continue under the amended rules may stop submitting.

    17. Resolving a dispute

    If the organizers become aware of a possible violation of these rules, they will notify you.

    Discuss it with them within five working days of that notice, with the aim of resolving it within ten days. The organizers’ decision on whether a violation occurred is final.

    If the matter is not discussed within five working days, or is not resolved within ten days, the organizers may disqualify the Entry, or ban the individual or team from future MecCog challenges as section 8 describes.

    18. Platform terms

    MecCog challenges run on the Hugging Face platform. Your Hugging Face account, the challenge Space, and anything you upload to it are governed by Hugging Face’s own terms of service and content policy, which you accept separately from these rules and which cover the relationship between you and the platform.

    These rules add the obligations above, which concern the challenge itself. They do not replace the platform terms. Where the two differ on a matter of platform use, the platform terms apply.

    Questions. The Community tab of this Space is where questions get asked and answered, and it is usually the fastest route, since someone has often been there before you. You can also join the conversation on Discord. For anything else, write to the organizers at e4admin@tunc.org.
